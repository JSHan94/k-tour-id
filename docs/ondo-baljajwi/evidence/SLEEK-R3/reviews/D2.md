## Interaction and IA assessment

### Discoverability and navigation depth

The first-run guide offers a conspicuous guest escape before any identity work. Nation selection leads to city map, the map announces that it is visual and points keyboard users to the list, and Map/List is a direct toggle. A fixed four-item primary navigation exposes ONDO, My Korea, Tables, and ID without burying the product's main domains. Place actions stay attached to the selected place sheet; JIT gates explain the needed proof at the moment of action rather than making verification a prerequisite for browsing.

Observed depth stays appropriate for task complexity:

- discovery: nation → city → list/map → place peek → detail;
- participation: Tables → table detail → join → chat;
- gated actions: originating surface → one explicit gate → exact origin;
- durable records: primary nav → My Korea or Tables → saved/joined record;
- experimental concepts: My Korea → Labs, with a clear acknowledgement boundary.

No reachable terminal state depended on a hidden gesture, icon-only mystery action, or non-semantic card. Full, ended, cancelled, unsupported, and locked rows remain selectable when an explanation is useful; unavailable actions are visibly disabled when no action is possible.

### Exact return and state continuity

Automated journeys proved every `RETURN` checkpoint. In the live browser, a place-first Tables entry retained the selected venue context and exposed both “return to place” and “browse all Tables.” A venue-first After19 probe preserved `data-venue-id=mois-68ca97d50aca6cb13438`; the canonical automated proof preserved `mois-0021cd596bc5b2a922ad`. Account completion returned to the same `table-seongsu-dinner` detail, then promoted it to the joined region and exposed chat.

Hard reload intentionally returned the transient shell to Nation instead of resurrecting an old modal. Durable state remained intact: opening Tables again restored the joined table, and reopening it restored the report receipt code. Save/My Korea, checkout/receipt/stamp, local-signal drafts, profile consent, After19 manual-off, and Labs receipt continuity were separately covered by the FL-001…018 suite.

### Focus, keyboard, pointer, and touch

The live nested Connect confirmation focused `report-reason`; `Shift+Tab` wrapped to `confirm-cancel`; `Escape` closed only the top confirmation and returned focus to `table-report`. During the confirmation, the main navigation was `inert` and `aria-hidden=true`, the chat sheet was suspended to a non-modal region, and exactly one exposed `aria-modal=true` alertdialog remained. Closing the nested layer restored the chat dialog as the one modal surface.

The same interaction was exercised by both `mobile-chromium` and `desktop-chromium`. Touch-sized controls, focus indicators, keyboard paths, scroll containment, serious/critical Axe results, and horizontal overflow guards passed across the 14 content families. Visible focus baselines showed the expected blue focus treatment rather than relying on colorless browser defaults.

### Rapid-input stress

At `390×844`, three concurrent activations of the After19 trigger produced at most one exposed prompt. A rapid Enter sequence either advanced the top gate or returned to the same map/venue; it did not leave an inert shell, stacked modal, orphan overlay, or lost venue. Repeating the trigger from an open place sheet restored that same place sheet without duplication.

In joined Connect chat, four rapid report-trigger activations converged to one valid chat state. A normal reopen produced one isolated alertdialog and correct initial focus. After choosing a reason, three rapid submit activations produced one report record and one receipt code; no duplicate record or second modal was created. Reload plus re-entry restored the joined Table and receipt. This supplements the frozen nested-modal regression test, which passed in both mobile and desktop projects.

### Responsive interaction and scroll

The full 45-case matrix was inspected at `360×800`, `390×844`, `430×932`, `768×1024`, `801×1000`, and `1440×1000`. A live browser resize through the same six sizes returned `scrollWidth === clientWidth` each time and retained the primary navigation. The `801` breakpoint changes the navigation and sheet composition without obscuring the active control. Mobile bottom navigation, tablet sheets, desktop rail navigation, dialog scrolling, sticky actions, and long KO copy showed no clipped decision control or competing scroll trap. English was also switched live at `360×800`, retaining no horizontal overflow and the four-item `Main navigation`.

### Recovery and CTA integrity

Visible recovery remains local to the failed task: map failure keeps the sourced list, save failure keeps the venue, join and image failures preserve the Table/chat, local-signal failure preserves the draft, checkout decline preserves stamp `9`, identity/payment failures preserve their return token, and bridge failure leaves assets unchanged. Retry actions repeat the same task rather than redirecting to a generic landing page. The five intentional N/A checkpoints are contract fallbacks or resume-time reevaluation, not missing recovery.

Across normal, failure, retry, cancel, return, locked, unsupported, empty, pending, and success states, every visible primary or secondary CTA inspected either changed the promised local state, opened its stated explanation, performed a documented external navigation, or returned to its labeled context. Dead CTA count: `0`.
